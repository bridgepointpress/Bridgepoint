# Bridge and Baseline — YouTube Content Research
**Date:** June 2026

---

## Channel Context (from existing docs)

- **Niche:** Guitar setup, ergonomics, and adaptive playing for adult guitarists aged 35–65
- **Audience:** Players fighting friction — arthritis, reduced grip, returning players, post-injury hands
- **Core format:** One friction point → PM insight → Fix Ladder ($0 / $6–20 / $25–100 / $100+) → before/after demo → 30 sec uninterrupted playing
- **Mission:** "Luxury is the absence of friction. Easier is not a shortcut — easier is the point."
- **Already scripted (Videos 1–9):** Setup basics, arthritis (PTI Ep 1), pawn shop rescue, barre chords, $4k mistake, light strings, ergonomics, identity video, PTI series intro

## Content Gaps and Underexplored Angles

1. **Carpal tunnel as a distinct condition** — forums are full of this question; no channel addresses it clearly and directly. High-priority gap.
2. **Seasonal/humidity content** — guitar setups drift in winter. Repeatable, searchable, and builds returning viewer habit.
3. **The luthier vs. DIY decision** — high emotional charge for this audience (cost anxiety + trust issues); the channel is perfectly positioned to answer it neutrally.
4. **"Viewer rescue" format** — invite a subscriber to send their guitar, document the full fix on camera. Builds community, generates social proof, creates shareable content.
5. **Playing during a flare-up** — most adaptive playing content is setup-focused; no one covers what to do on a bad hand day.
6. **Guitar size and body fit** — short-scale, parlour, and 3/4 guitars are covered in gear content but almost never framed as a comfort/ergonomics fix. Strong channel fit.
7. **One-handed and partial-limb playing** — Django is already in the channel DNA. An episode here would be the most shareable PTI video and draw coverage beyond the guitar niche.

## Research Update — 20 July 2026

*This section is additive — see "New Video Ideas by Series" above for the June 2026 backlog, which remains current. Ideas below avoid duplicating carpal tunnel, humidity/seasonal drift, luthier-vs-DIY, viewer rescue, flare-up, short-scale/body-fit, one-handed/Django, silent practice, strap height, and existing support-system topics, all of which are already planned.*

### Trending Topics & Keywords (checked against current search/market signal)

- **Fret buzz, guitar action, and neck relief** remain the highest-volume entry-point searches in the niche — confirms the existing "Guitar Setup Basics" list is still correctly prioritised, no change needed
- **"Returning to guitar after years away"** — strong and growing search interest tied to adult hobbyists and retirees; most existing content addresses motivation/practice psychology, almost none addresses the *instrument-side* question (what actually changed while it sat)
- **Small hands / short fingers — nut width and neck profile** — active, specific search terms (41–42mm vs. standard 43mm nut width; C-shape vs. D-shape profiles) that are distinct from both arthritis and short-scale coverage; this is an anatomical fit question, not a medical or size-of-instrument one
- **Ergonomic guitar design and short-scale instruments** are both independently flagged as rising 2026 YouTube niches — validates the channel's existing angle rather than surfacing something new
- **Assistive fretting devices (ChordBuddy-type products)** — a distinct, actively searched product category with no channel coverage; sits alongside setup fixes rather than replacing them
- **DIY guitar kit building** — a visible 2026 trend (kit sellers reporting strong volume); relevant here as a full-control, build-to-your-own-specs option rather than a hobbyist-craft angle
- **Seated electric guitar support** — most ergonomic support content and product searches skew classical/acoustic; electric-specific seated comfort is comparatively thin

### New Video Ideas

**1. Why Your Chords Feel Too Far Apart**
- *Friction:* A chord stretch that never gets easier with practice, regardless of technique.
- *Fix angle:* Nut width and string spacing — a spec mismatch, not a skill gap.
- *Fix Ladder:* Free — thumb position and wrist angle adjustments to shorten the reach · Affordable (£5–20) — lighter-gauge strings, which sit closer together · Quality upgrade (£25–100) — a professional nut re-slot to narrow string spacing on the current guitar · Premium (£100+) — a guitar built with a genuinely narrower nut width from the start
- *Adaptive relevance:* Directly useful for players with small hands, short fingers, or reduced reach from joint stiffness — reframes a common source of self-blame as a measurable spec.

**2. The Guitar That Sat for Three Years: What Actually Changed**
- *Friction:* Coming back to guitar after a long break and not knowing whether the difficulty is rust or a guitar that drifted out of setup while it waited.
- *Fix angle:* Baseline reset — check the instrument before assuming it's the player.
- *Fix Ladder:* Free — full visual and feel check on action, tuning stability, string age · Affordable (£5–20) — a fresh set of strings, often the single biggest change after long storage · Quality upgrade (£25–100) — a full setup to reset neck relief and action after months of humidity drift · Premium (£100+) — a structural inspection if the case wasn't climate-controlled
- *Adaptive relevance:* Removes the "my hands got worse" narrative that returning players often default to, and replaces it with something measurable.

**3. Sitting Down With an Electric Guitar: The Support Nobody Sells You On**
- *Friction:* Most practice happens seated, but seated electric comfort gets none of the attention strap height and standing posture get.
- *Fix angle:* Seated-specific ergonomic support, evaluated the same way as the channel's existing (classical-leaning) support comparison.
- *Fix Ladder:* Free — a cushion or rolled towel repositioning the body against the leg · Affordable (£5–20) — a strap resized specifically for seated height · Quality upgrade (£25–100) — a dedicated leg-rest or lap stand built for electrics · Premium (£100+) — a contoured or chambered body chosen with seated comfort as a spec, not an afterthought
- *Adaptive relevance:* Directly relevant to players who practice seated by necessity — fatigue, balance, or limited standing time.

**4. Should You Build Your Own Guitar? The Kit Question for Adult Players**
- *Friction:* No guitar on the market fits every spec at once, and swapping one component at a time on a budget guitar has limits.
- *Fix angle:* A kit build lets scale length, neck width, and nut height all be chosen up front, for players with more time than money and a specific fit in mind.
- *Fix Ladder:* Free — measuring your own hand span and reach before spending anything · Entry kit (~£150) — basic build, full control over core specs · Mid kit (~£300) — better hardware and finish options · Premium kit (£600+) — near-custom spec control
- *Note:* This doesn't map to the usual £0–£100+ ladder cleanly — worth saying on camera that a build is a real time-and-money commitment, not a shortcut, consistent with never implying all fixes are small or quick.

**5. ChordBuddy and Fretting Aids: An Honest Look**
- *Friction:* For players where fretting itself is the painful part, even after every setup change, no even-handed video says what an assistive fretting device is actually for.
- *Fix angle:* Evaluate the product category on its own terms, alongside (not instead of) setup fixes.
- *Fix Ladder:* Free — open or alternate tunings that reduce full-hand chord shapes · Affordable (£5–20) — partial-capo technique · Quality upgrade (£25–100) — an assistive fretting device · Premium — not applicable; this category tops out mid-ladder
- *Gate before producing:* Requires the Mode 2 checklist and personal use of the specific product before any on-camera claim or recommendation — this is a brand-evaluation task, not yet a scripted idea.

### Gaps and Underexplored Angles

1. **Anatomical fit (small hands, short fingers, nut width, neck profile)** is entirely absent from the current backlog. It sits next to arthritis coverage but is a distinct mismatch — not medical, not about instrument size — and fits the channel's "optimum for how you play" thesis directly.
2. **Assistive fretting hardware as a product category** has zero current coverage or Fix Ladder placement. It's adjacent to but distinct from setup fixes, and needs a Mode 2 evaluation pass before any specific brand is featured on camera.
3. **Returning players** are currently addressed only through practice psychology and motivation; no video checks the instrument itself after a long layoff, even though that's a concrete, filmable, before/after topic.
4. **Seated electric-specific ergonomics** is thin industry-wide, and the channel's existing support-system comparison leans classical/acoustic — a gap the channel is well positioned to fill.
5. **Full-control builds (kits)** are outside the current ladder's usual range and would need honest framing about time and cost, but represent a genuine premium-tier option for the Comfortable Player persona that isn't currently on the backlog.
