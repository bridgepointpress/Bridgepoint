## Content Gaps and Underexplored Angles

1. **Carpal tunnel as a distinct condition** — forums are full of this question; no channel addresses it clearly and directly. High-priority gap.
2. **Seasonal/humidity content** — guitar setups drift in winter. Repeatable, searchable, and builds returning viewer habit.
3. **The luthier vs. DIY decision** — high emotional charge for this audience (cost anxiety + trust issues); the channel is perfectly positioned to answer it neutrally.
4. **"Viewer rescue" format** — invite a subscriber to send their guitar, document the full fix on camera. Builds community, generates social proof, creates shareable content.
5. **Playing during a flare-up** — most adaptive playing content is setup-focused; no one covers what to do on a bad hand day.
6. **Guitar size and body fit** — short-scale, parlour, and 3/4 guitars are covered in gear content but almost never framed as a comfort/ergonomics fix. Strong channel fit.
7. **One-handed and partial-limb playing** — Django is already in the channel DNA. An episode here would be the most shareable PTI video and draw coverage beyond the guitar niche.

## New Video Ideas (genuinely unclaimed by the backlog)

**1. Trigger Finger and the Guitar**
- Friction: a finger catches or locks mid-fret, most common at the base of the finger, often dismissed as clumsiness rather than named as trigger finger.
- Fix Ladder: £0 — retune to open tuning to reduce repetitive flexion on the affected finger · £5–20 — looser-tension strings (D'Addario EXL120) · £25–100 — Graph Tech nut/saddle drop-in to reduce fretting pressure needed · £100+ — professional setup plus a scale-length change if flexion load is the driver.
- Audience appeal: Medium-high. Almost no dedicated content exists; strong forum search signal per earlier research.

**2. Carpal Tunnel and Guitar: What the Setup Can Fix (And What It Can't)**
- Friction: numbness/tingling down the thumb-side fingers during or after playing, often blamed on "bad technique" when wrist angle from action height is a contributing factor.
- Fix Ladder: £0 — neutral wrist position check + strap height adjustment · £5–20 — lighter strings to cut required grip force · £25–100 — nut/saddle work to lower action, reducing the wrist extension needed to fret cleanly · £100+ — full comfort setup package.
- Audience appeal: High. Confirmed active search demand; distinct mechanism from arthritis keeps it from cannibalising Video 2/48.

**3. Neuropathy: When Fingers Go Numb**
- Friction: reduced or absent sensation makes it hard to judge fretting pressure — too little buzzes, too much strains — independent of grip strength.
- Fix Ladder: £0 — visual fret-buzz check to replace feel-based feedback · £5–20 — heavier-gauge strings for more tactile feedback where numbness (not weakness) is the issue · £25–100 — professional intonation and action set to remove guesswork · £100+ — short-scale or wide-neck guitar if spacing is the real variable.
- Audience appeal: Medium. Smaller but underserved audience (diabetic and chemo-related neuropathy); strong differentiator for the Playing Through It series.

**4. Raynaud's and Cold Hands: Playing When Your Fingers Won't Cooperate**
- Friction: reduced circulation makes fingers stiff, slow, or painful in cold rooms or air-conditioned spaces — a situational/seasonal constraint rather than a permanent condition.
- Fix Ladder: £0 — warm-up routine and room/hand-position adjustment before playing · £5–20 — fingerless gloves or hand warmers as a pre-session ritual · £25–100 — lighter strings to reduce the force needed while circulation is still returning · £100+ — none needed; this is a low-cost-tier friction point, worth saying so explicitly.
- Audience appeal: Low-medium reach, but essentially zero competing content — a genuine white-space video with strong shareability in Raynaud's/chronic illness communities.

**5. Menopause, Joint Stiffness, and Guitar**
- Friction: perimenopausal and menopausal joint/tendon changes (including a rise in trigger finger and frozen-shoulder-type stiffness) affect a large share of the channel's own demographic but are never named in guitar content.
- Fix Ladder: £0 — session-length and warm-up adjustment on stiff days · £5–20 — lighter strings · £25–100 — comfort setup package · £100+ — short-scale guitar if reduced reach is the pattern.
- Audience appeal: Medium-high and highly differentiated — no guitar channel currently names this condition, despite it directly overlapping the 35–65 audience.

**6. Long COVID Fatigue: The Guitar Session That Still Counts**
- Friction: post-viral fatigue makes even a short practice session feel disproportionately draining, and standard "just do 15 minutes" advice doesn't account for days when even that is too much.
- Fix Ladder: £0 — a scaled-down 5-minute version of the existing sprint concept, seated, no standing or long sessions · £5–20 — lightest available strings to cut physical cost per note · £25–100 — comfort setup package · £100+ — not typically needed; the fix here is mostly structural, not financial.
- Audience appeal: Medium. Timely, underserved, and a natural extension of Video 45's flare-management logic without duplicating it — should be positioned as a companion episode, not a replacement.
