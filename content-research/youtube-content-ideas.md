**Date:** June 2026

---

## Channel Context (from existing docs)

- **Niche:** Guitar setup, ergonomics, and adaptive playing for adult guitarists aged 35–65
- **Audience:** Players fighting friction — arthritis, reduced grip, returning players, post-injury hands
- **Core format:** One friction point → PM insight → Fix Ladder ($0 / $6–20 / $25–100 / $100+) → before/after demo → 30 sec uninterrupted playing
- **Mission:** "Luxury is the absence of friction. Easier is not a shortcut — easier is the point."
- **Already scripted (Videos 1–9):** Setup basics, arthritis (PTI Ep 1), pawn shop rescue, barre chords, $4k mistake, light strings, ergonomics, identity video, PTI series intro

- Spatial audio / headphone amp comparison (Boss Waza Air, Fender Mustang Micro trending in silent practice)

## Content Gaps and Underexplored Angles

1. **Carpal tunnel as a distinct condition** — forums are full of this question; no channel addresses it clearly and directly. High-priority gap.
2. **Seasonal/humidity content** — guitar setups drift in winter. Repeatable, searchable, and builds returning viewer habit.
3. **The luthier vs. DIY decision** — high emotional charge for this audience (cost anxiety + trust issues); the channel is perfectly positioned to answer it neutrally.
4. **"Viewer rescue" format** — invite a subscriber to send their guitar, document the full fix on camera. Builds community, generates social proof, creates shareable content.
5. **Playing during a flare-up** — most adaptive playing content is setup-focused; no one covers what to do on a bad hand day.
6. **Guitar size and body fit** — short-scale, parlour, and 3/4 guitars are covered in gear content but almost never framed as a comfort/ergonomics fix. Strong channel fit.
7. **One-handed and partial-limb playing** — Django is already in the channel DNA. An episode here would be the most shareable PTI video and draw coverage beyond the guitar niche.

That's a live cultural moment the channel can speak into directly, in its own tone (no hype, no "fighting" language).

### New video ideas

**1. Left-Handed on a Right-Handed Guitar: What Your Actual Options Are**
- Friction: a left-handed player learned on, inherited, or was gifted a right-handed guitar and has never been told whether restringing, flipping, or staying put is the right call for them.
- Fix angle: setup framing with no assumed dominant hand.
- Fix Ladder: Free — play right-handed as-is, technique adapts either way → Affordable £5–20 — restring the same guitar upside down → Quality upgrade £25–100 — re-cut nut and saddle for the flipped string order → Premium £100+ — dedicated left-handed short-scale build.
- Why it's worth doing: genuinely low-competition on guitar YouTube, and it's a direct extension of the channel's "no assumed body" position — this just hasn't been said out loud for handedness yet.

**2. The Off-Guitar Hand Warm-Up: Five Minutes Before You Touch a String**
- Friction: hands feel stiff or reluctant before a session even starts, so the guitar stays in its case.
- Fix angle: a short mobility routine done before picking up the instrument, not a playing exercise.
- Fix Ladder: Free — a five-move stretch sequence → Affordable — therapy putty or a stress ball → Quality upgrade — a dedicated hand exerciser → Premium — a one-off occupational therapy consult, framed as an option, not a requirement.
- Adaptive relevance: draws directly on Arthritis Checklist Section 1A (pain that worsens vs. improves with movement) and the music-therapy research above — gives the video a real citation instead of a general claim.

**3. Short-Scale Guitars Aren't Beginner Guitars Anymore**
- Friction: an adult player assumes a short-scale guitar signals they haven't progressed, so they stay on a full-scale neck that doesn't fit their hand.
- Fix angle: myth-busting, anchored in the 2026 shift in how short-scale instruments are talked about.
- Fix Ladder: Free — try a short-scale in a shop before ruling it out → Affordable — a used short-scale electric → Quality upgrade — a new in-house-brand short-scale model (see Short Scale Guitars 2026 doc) → Premium — a boutique short-scale build.
- Why now: this is a title that couldn't have worked the same way three years ago — the "beginner" stigma is the thing being actively dismantled in 2026 coverage, which makes the hook timely rather than evergreen-generic.

**4. Stroke Recovery and the Guitar: Playing During a Non-Linear Comeback**
- Friction: someone recovering from a stroke assumes guitar is off the table until both hands "work like before."
- Fix angle: distinct from the existing One-Handed and Adaptive Guitar episode (Video 35), which is framed around permanent adaptation. This one is about the recovery arc specifically — playing during and after rehab, progress that isn't a straight line.
- Fix Ladder: Free — open tuning with a single-hand strum pattern → Affordable £5–20 — partial capo → Quality upgrade £25–100 — light-gauge strings matched to whichever hand is carrying the fretting load that week → Premium £100+ — an adaptive strap or support system.
- Note: stroke recovery is already named in the Playing Through It series mission but hasn't had its own episode — this closes that specific gap rather than duplicating Video 35.

**5. Setting Up a Guitar for Low Vision**
- Friction: a player with low vision or blindness relies entirely on feel, but standard fret markers and setup assume sight.
- Fix angle: tactile and non-visual setup adaptations.
- Fix Ladder: Free — feel-based fret-counting technique → Affordable — stick-on tactile fret markers → Quality upgrade — luminescent side markers plus a raised nut reference point → Premium — custom tactile fretboard work.
- Why it's worth doing: this appears to be entirely unaddressed on guitar YouTube. Small audience, but it's the clearest possible demonstration that the channel means it when it says there's no assumed default body.

**6. What 2026 Guitar Journalism Just Started Saying Out Loud**
- Friction: a player reads a mainstream piece describing aging guitarists as needing to train "like athletes" and doesn't know what that actually means for their own hands.
- Fix angle: a reaction/commentary video that takes a real, current press moment (the July 2026 Guitar World piece on arthritis and aging guitarists) and translates the "athlete" framing into the channel's existing PM language — periodization becomes iterative improvement, load management becomes the Fix Ladder.
- Structure note: this is an identity/insight video like Video 8 — carries a tools-of-the-trade ladder rather than a traditional Fix Ladder.
- Why now: the channel gets to point at outside validation of its own thesis, in its own voice, while the moment is current.

### A format gap, not just a content gap

Current Shorts thinking (per the Format Notes above) treats Shorts only as cut-downs of long-form videos. 2026 platform behaviour rewards channels that also post Shorts as their own stream — and per the research above, doing both is linked to higher long-form watch time, not just Shorts views on their own. Worth considering a recurring Shorts-only strand — a single measurement-to-relief moment, no talking, no title card — separate from the long-form before/after clips already planned. Not a video idea so much as a publishing habit worth testing.
