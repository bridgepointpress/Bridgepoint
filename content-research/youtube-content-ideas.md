**Date:** June 2026

## Channel Context (from existing docs)

- **Niche:** Guitar setup, ergonomics, and adaptive playing for adult guitarists aged 35–65
- **Audience:** Players fighting friction — arthritis, reduced grip, returning players, post-injury hands
- **Core format:** One friction point → PM insight → Fix Ladder ($0 / $6–20 / $25–100 / $100+) → before/after demo → 30 sec uninterrupted playing
- **Mission:** "Luxury is the absence of friction. Easier is not a shortcut — easier is the point."
- **Already scripted (Videos 1–9):** Setup basics, arthritis (PTI Ep 1), pawn shop rescue, barre chords, $4k mistake, light strings, ergonomics, identity video, PTI series intro

## Content Gaps and Underexplored Angles

1. **Carpal tunnel as a distinct condition** — forums are full of this question; no channel addresses it clearly and directly. High-priority gap.
2. **Seasonal/humidity content** — guitar setups drift in winter. Repeatable, searchable, and builds returning viewer habit.
3. **The luthier vs. DIY decision** — high emotional charge for this audience (cost anxiety + trust issues); the channel is perfectly positioned to answer it neutrally.
4. **"Viewer rescue" format** — invite a subscriber to send their guitar, document the full fix on camera. Builds community, generates social proof, creates shareable content.
5. **Playing during a flare-up** — most adaptive playing content is setup-focused; no one covers what to do on a bad hand day.
6. **Guitar size and body fit** — short-scale, parlour, and 3/4 guitars are covered in gear content but almost never framed as a comfort/ergonomics fix. Strong channel fit.
7. **One-handed and partial-limb playing** — Django is already in the channel DNA. An episode here would be the most shareable PTI video and draw coverage beyond the guitar niche.

## Research Update — 31 July 2026

### What's New Since June

The June research pass already claimed most of the high-volume core (fret buzz, action, arthritis, silent practice, short-scale). This pass focuses on what's shifted since then and what's still unclaimed — not re-covering ground already logged above.

### Trending Topics & Keywords (new signal)

- **"does playing guitar make arthritis worse"** — a myth-adjacent search cluster showing up around arthritis content; current answer online is scattered and reassurance-first rather than setup-first. Directly in the channel's lane.
- **Silicone finger caps / fingertip protectors** — surfacing repeatedly in arthritis-adjacent searches as a named product category, distinct from the string-lubricant angle already logged in June. No Fix Ladder placement for it yet.
- **Open tunings (Open D / Open G) as a comfort strategy** — recurring in arthritis and joint-pain discussions as a way to simplify chord shapes, but framed everywhere else as a "trick" rather than a legitimate permanent approach — a framing gap this channel is built to close.
- **2026 headphone-amp refresh cycle** — Fender Mustang Micro Plus, Boss Katana 50 Gen 3, and Vox Amplug 3 have all shipped updates since the June comparison video was scoped (USB-C charging, Bluetooth backing tracks). The existing "Headphone Amps Compared" idea is still accurate in structure but the specific models need a refresh pass before scripting.
- **"Are budget guitars actually better now?"** — factory setup quality and build consistency on entry-level instruments is a live discussion point in 2026 gear coverage; ties directly into the Budget Guitar Rescue series with a fresh hook (the premise the series was built on — cheap guitars need rescuing — is itself getting weaker, which is a story).
- **Slim-taper neck vs. short-scale for hand comfort** — current gear coverage is quietly correcting the assumption that shorter scale automatically means easier: a slim neck profile on a full-scale instrument is being framed as the better fix for some players. This complicates (and strengthens) the channel's existing short-scale content.

### New Video Ideas (Mode 1 — Raw List)

**1. Working title: "Does Playing Guitar Make Arthritis Worse? (What's Actually True)"**
- *Friction:* Some players hesitate to pick the guitar back up because they're not sure if playing will damage already-painful joints.
- *Fix angle:* Myth-correction + setup framing — the honest answer is nuanced, and the channel can hold that nuance without giving false reassurance.
- *Fix Ladder sketch:* Free — pain-pattern self-check (worsens vs. improves with movement, from the Arthritis Checklist) / Affordable — lighter strings, warm-up routine / Quality upgrade — professional setup lowering action and effort / Premium — bespoke setup or instrument change if pain is structural, not just effort-based

**2. Working title: "Silicone Finger Caps: Do They Actually Help?"**
- *Friction:* Fingertip pain from string pressure discourages practice before calluses or comfort with tone-shaping technique develop.
- *Fix angle:* Honest product test — where finger caps genuinely help (early-stage pain, short sessions) versus where a setup fix would serve better (chronic buzz, high action).
- *Fix Ladder sketch:* Free — technique adjustment (lighter fretting pressure) / Affordable — silicone finger caps, a few pounds per set / Quality upgrade — lighter string gauge paired with caps / Premium — full setup so caps become optional rather than necessary

**3. Working title: "Open Tunings Aren't a Trick — They're a Real Way to Play"**
- *Friction:* Players with limited finger independence or reach are told alternate tunings are a shortcut for people who "can't do it properly."
- *Fix angle:* Reframes open D/open G as a permanent, legitimate approach — fewer simultaneous finger placements, same musical range.
- *Fix Ladder sketch:* Free — retune existing strings, no new gear / Affordable — a capo to shift open-tuning keys quickly / Quality upgrade — a dedicated open-tuning guitar left tuned that way / Premium — a second instrument built or set up specifically for open-tuning comfort

**4. Working title: "The Budget Guitar Rescue Premise Is Getting Weaker (Here's the Data)"**
- *Friction:* Viewers assume every sub-£150 guitar needs the same triage the channel has documented — but factory setups have quietly improved.
- *Fix angle:* Turns a series update into content — tests a current-year budget guitar out of the box against the Video 3 rescue baseline, honest about what's changed and what still needs fixing.
- *Fix Ladder sketch:* Free — out-of-box playability check before assuming a rescue is needed / Affordable — the handful of fixes that are still consistently needed / Quality upgrade — where a fresh 2026 model still falls short / Premium — when to skip the rescue and buy up a tier instead

**5. Working title: "Slim Neck or Short Scale? The Comfort Fix Nobody Explains Clearly"**
- *Friction:* Players assume short-scale is automatically the easier option, without knowing a slim-taper neck on a full-scale guitar might solve the same problem with less tonal compromise.
- *Fix angle:* Direct comparison, hands-on, measured — not a buying guide, a decision framework.
- *Fix Ladder sketch:* Free — measure your own hand span and current neck profile before buying anything / Affordable — a neck-profile-only swap on an existing bolt-on guitar, where feasible / Quality upgrade — a slim-neck full-scale instrument / Premium — a proper short-scale instrument built for tone as well as comfort

**6. Working title: "2026 Headphone Amps Refresh: What Changed and What's Worth Upgrading"**
- *Friction:* Viewers who bought a silent-practice rig a year or two ago don't know if the newer models are a meaningful upgrade or a marketing refresh.
- *Fix angle:* Direct refresh of the June comparison idea, scoped to what's actually new (USB-C charging, Bluetooth backing tracks) rather than re-reviewing the category from scratch.
- *Fix Ladder sketch:* Free — check whether your current rig's limitation is actually solved by a new feature / Affordable — Vox Amplug 3 / Quality upgrade — Fender Mustang Micro Plus / Premium — Boss Katana 50 Gen 3 as a practice amp that doubles as a small gigging amp

**7. Working title: "The Guitar That's Fine, Actually (When Not to Fix Anything)"**
- *Friction:* An audience trained by fix-focused content can start hunting for problems that aren't there, adding friction of its own — anxiety, unnecessary spending.
- *Fix angle:* A deliberate counter-episode: walks through a guitar that's already well-matched to its player and explains why no further fix is warranted, using the same ROI framework in reverse.
- *Fix Ladder sketch:* Free — a "good enough" checklist to confirm the instrument doesn't need work / Affordable — n/a, framed honestly / Quality upgrade — n/a, framed honestly / Premium — n/a, framed honestly (the point of the video is that not every tier applies every time)

### Content Gaps and Underexplored Angles (additions to June's list)

1. **The "does it make it worse" reassurance gap** — nearly all existing arthritis-and-guitar content online defaults to blanket reassurance rather than the channel's honest, setup-first nuance. High-priority, low-competition gap.
2. **Finger caps as a named, distinct product category** — currently folded into general "aids" content elsewhere; no channel gives it a fair, specific Fix Ladder treatment.
3. **A deliberate "no fix needed" episode** — the entire catalogue (June and current) is fix-forward; one calibration episode would reinforce that the channel isn't selling problems, which strengthens trust with both personas.
4. **Series self-correction as content** — using the Budget Guitar Rescue premise's own aging as the hook for a new episode is an underused format in this niche generally, not just on this channel.
