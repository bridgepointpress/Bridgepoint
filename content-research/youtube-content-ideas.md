**Date:** June 2026

## Channel Context (from existing docs)

- **Niche:** Guitar setup, ergonomics, and adaptive playing for adult guitarists aged 35–65
- **Audience:** Players fighting friction — arthritis, reduced grip, returning players, post-injury hands
- **Core format:** One friction point → PM insight → Fix Ladder ($0 / $6–20 / $25–100 / $100+) → before/after demo → 30 sec uninterrupted playing
- **Mission:** "Luxury is the absence of friction. Easier is not a shortcut — easier is the point."
- **Already scripted (Videos 1–9):** Setup basics, arthritis (PTI Ep 1), pawn shop rescue, barre chords, $4k mistake, light strings, ergonomics, identity video, PTI series intro

## Content Gaps and Underexplored Angles

1. **Carpal tunnel as a distinct condition** — forums are full of this question; no channel addresses it clearly and directly. High-priority gap.
2. **Seasonal/humidity content** — guitar setups drift in winter. Repeatable, searchable, and builds returning viewer habit.
3. **The luthier vs. DIY decision** — high emotional charge for this audience (cost anxiety + trust issues); the channel is perfectly positioned to answer it neutrally.
4. **"Viewer rescue" format** — invite a subscriber to send their guitar, document the full fix on camera. Builds community, generates social proof, creates shareable content.
5. **Playing during a flare-up** — most adaptive playing content is setup-focused; no one covers what to do on a bad hand day.
6. **Guitar size and body fit** — short-scale, parlour, and 3/4 guitars are covered in gear content but almost never framed as a comfort/ergonomics fix. Strong channel fit.
7. **One-handed and partial-limb playing** — Django is already in the channel DNA. An episode here would be the most shareable PTI video and draw coverage beyond the guitar niche.

## Research Update — 13 September 2026

**Sources checked:** the live 52-video production backlog (`Bridge_and_Baseline_Final_v7.xlsx`), the Channel Ops Guide, and the ideas already logged above from June 2026. This update only lists angles that are genuinely new against both — no repeats of Carpal Tunnel, Tendinitis, Trigger Finger, Neuropathy, Shoulder/Neck Pain, Flare-Up management, the Playing Through It episode slate, silent-practice, or the practice-systems ideas already on file.

### Trending Topics & Keywords

- **Arthritis-and-guitar is having a news moment.** Keith Richards, Joe Perry, and Alex Lifeson have all been covered in 2026 press discussing how arthritis changed their playing. This is a search-traffic tailwind for the channel's existing Arthritis and Playing Through It videos — no new video needed, but worth a passing, non-hype mention ("you're in the same company as players who've been doing this for fifty years") rather than a celebrity-chasing video, which would break the channel's no-hype guardrail.
- **Vision changes and guitar** — forums (JustinGuitar community, Gear Forum, TalkBass) have real, recurring threads on seeing fret markers and playing in low light, with no clear beginner-friendly video answering it directly. High-contrast/reflective/glow fret markers are a known but under-videoed fix.
- **Raynaud's phenomenon and guitar** — genuine clinical literature exists (vascular response distinct from arthritis, linked to string vibration and cold), plus scattered forum threads, but zero dedicated YouTube coverage found. Clear content gap with medical backing.
- **"Guitar for beginners over 50"** and similar evergreen buyer's-guide search terms benefit from periodic refreshes with a "(2026)" framing rather than a wholly new topic — a maintenance task, not a new idea.

### New Video Ideas (Raw List)

**1. Cold Hands and the Guitar: When It's Circulation, Not Arthritis**
- Friction: fingers go stiff, pale, or numb before playing even starts, and the player assumes it's just "a bad night" rather than a distinct, manageable response.
- Fix angle: warm-up protocol + approach, clearly separated from the arthritis video's setup focus — this is a different mechanism.
- Fix Ladder: Free — warm hands under running water or against a radiator before picking up the guitar, longer gentle warm-up. Affordable (£5–20) — fingerless circulation gloves or hand warmers. Quality upgrade (£25–100) — lighter-gauge strings to reduce load while hands are still warming. Premium (£100+) — a small heater kept at the practice spot, framed as removing warm-up time as a reason to skip a session.

**2. Setting Up a Guitar for Eyes That Have Changed**
- Friction: the player still knows the shapes, but the fretboard itself has become hard to see — a vision problem, not a hand or technique problem.
- Fix angle: lighting and fretboard contrast, a genuinely new lens distinct from every hand-focused Playing Through It episode.
- Fix Ladder: Free — reposition practice lighting to rake across the fretboard rather than overhead. Affordable (£5–20) — adhesive high-contrast or glow-in-the-dark fret markers. Quality upgrade (£25–100) — an articulating clip lamp with adjustable colour temperature. Premium (£100+) — factoring visible binding and fretboard contrast into the next guitar purchase.

**3. Restringing When Your Hands Can't Pinch and Wind Anymore**
- Friction: changing strings takes repeated pinching and winding that some players' hands can no longer do, so guitars sit unplayed with dead strings rather than get restrung.
- Fix angle: distinct from the existing light-strings video — this is about the mechanical act of restringing itself, not string choice.
- Fix Ladder: Free — a bracing technique that uses leverage instead of pinch grip. Affordable (£5–20) — a basic peg winder. Quality upgrade (£25–100) — an electric string winder. Premium (£100+) — locking tuners installed, which remove most of the winding entirely.

**4. Fingerstyle Nail Care When Trimming and Filing Hurts**
- Friction: fingerstyle depends on nail shape and condition, but for players with arthritis or reduced fine motor control in the fingers themselves, trimming and filing becomes its own painful task before the guitar is even picked up.
- Fix angle: grooming-as-friction, an angle no existing video touches.
- Fix Ladder: Free — a filing order and direction that needs less grip. Affordable (£5–20) — an ergonomic large-handle file and clippers. Quality upgrade (£25–100) — nail hardener plus a glass file set to reduce filing frequency. Premium (£100+) — a set of well-fitted fingerpicks as a permanent, legitimate alternative to natural nails.

**5. The Trigger Capo vs. the Squeeze Capo: Which One Your Hand Can Actually Use**
- Friction: standard squeeze capos assume a strong pinch grip; players without one often give up on capo use entirely rather than switching capo types.
- Fix angle: a focused gear comparison that extends the channel's existing partial-capo strategy into a dedicated, grip-specific buying guide.
- Fix Ladder: Free — using the palm or heel of the hand to close a capo instead of a two-finger pinch. Affordable (£10–20) — a trigger-style capo. Quality upgrade (£25–45) — an auto-tension, one-hand capo. Premium (£50+) — a small set of partial capos covering multiple tunings.

**6. Practicing With Hearing Changes: Protecting What You Have Left**
- Friction: tinnitus or age-related hearing loss leads players to either avoid practicing, for fear of making it worse, or crank the volume to compensate — both of which mean less actual playing.
- Fix angle: protecting the player's hearing, distinct from the existing silent-practice content, which is about not disturbing others.
- Fix Ladder: Free — playing acoustic at a slight distance, or damping the soundhole to soften attack transients. Affordable (£10–20) — flat-response musician's earplugs rather than standard foam. Quality upgrade (£25–60) — a properly sized entry-level musician earplug set. Premium (£100+) — custom-molded earplugs from an audiologist.

**7. Carrying the Guitar Without Straining Your Back**
- Friction: lifting and carrying a hard case — into a car, up stairs — is often a bigger daily barrier than anything about actually playing.
- Fix angle: transport and storage strain, distinct from the existing "heavy guitar while playing" and wall-mount videos.
- Fix Ladder: Free — a two-handed lift technique that avoids twisting. Affordable (£15–25) — a padded gig bag with backpack-style straps. Quality upgrade (£40–80) — a wheeled or ergonomic backpack-style hard case. Premium (£100+) — a rolling flight-style case.

**8. Left-Handed and Cross-Dominant Setup: What Actually Needs to Change**
- Friction: left-handed and cross-dominant players are often told to "just learn right-handed," and rarely get straightforward setup guidance once they choose a reversed or left-handed instrument.
- Fix angle: a setup-geometry topic the backlog has never addressed.
- Fix Ladder: Free — trial-restringing a right-handed guitar reversed to test fit before buying anything. Affordable (£15–30) — a replacement nut cut for reversed stringing. Quality upgrade (£40–100) — a purpose-built budget left-handed guitar. Premium (£150+) — a full setup on a true left-handed instrument, nut, saddle, and bracing all built for it.

### Content Gaps and Underexplored Angles

1. **Vision changes** — zero coverage anywhere in the backlog or prior research, despite active forum discussion and an easy, cheap fix set.
2. **Hearing changes** — the channel has silent practice (protecting others) but nothing on protecting the player's own hearing.
3. **Circulation and temperature (Raynaud's, cold hands)** — a distinct physical mechanism from arthritis that the channel has never separated out, with genuine clinical backing.
4. **Fine-motor grooming friction** (nail care) — adjacent to but not covered by any existing setup or arthritis video.
5. **Transport and storage strain** — the channel covers playing ergonomics and practice-space setup, but not the lifting and carrying that happens before the guitar is even out of the case.
6. **Left-handed and cross-dominant setup** — entirely absent from the 52-video backlog.
