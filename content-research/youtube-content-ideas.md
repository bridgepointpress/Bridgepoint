**Date:** June 2026

## Channel Context (from existing docs)

- **Niche:** Guitar setup, ergonomics, and adaptive playing for adult guitarists aged 35–65
- **Audience:** Players fighting friction — arthritis, reduced grip, returning players, post-injury hands
- **Core format:** One friction point → PM insight → Fix Ladder ($0 / $6–20 / $25–100 / $100+) → before/after demo → 30 sec uninterrupted playing
- **Mission:** "Luxury is the absence of friction. Easier is not a shortcut — easier is the point."
- **Already scripted (Videos 1–9):** Setup basics, arthritis (PTI Ep 1), pawn shop rescue, barre chords, $4k mistake, light strings, ergonomics, identity video, PTI series intro

## Content Gaps and Underexplored Angles

1. **Carpal tunnel as a distinct condition** — forums are full of this question; no channel addresses it clearly and directly. High-priority gap.
2. **Seasonal/humidity content** — guitar setups drift in winter. Repeatable, searchable, and builds returning viewer habit.
3. **The luthier vs. DIY decision** — high emotional charge for this audience (cost anxiety + trust issues); the channel is perfectly positioned to answer it neutrally.
4. **"Viewer rescue" format** — invite a subscriber to send their guitar, document the full fix on camera. Builds community, generates social proof, creates shareable content.
5. **Playing during a flare-up** — most adaptive playing content is setup-focused; no one covers what to do on a bad hand day.
6. **Guitar size and body fit** — short-scale, parlour, and 3/4 guitars are covered in gear content but almost never framed as a comfort/ergonomics fix. Strong channel fit.
7. **One-handed and partial-limb playing** — Django is already in the channel DNA. An episode here would be the most shareable PTI video and draw coverage beyond the guitar niche.

## Trending Topics & Keywords (new signals since June)

- **Keith Richards' arthritis interview (March 2026)** — widely syndicated quote about deliberately playing fewer notes and "finding other ways around" a physical limitation. High relevance, currently ownable — no guitar-comfort channel has turned it into a teaching moment.
- **Parkinson's + guitar** — real clinical backing (the *GuitarPD* pilot study on nontraditional guitar instruction, published via NIH/PMC) and an active Parkinson's UK forum thread of guitarists asking how to keep playing. Zero mainstream guitar-YouTube coverage found.
- **Multi-scale / fanned-fret guitars** — 2026 trend pieces frame fanned frets explicitly as a wrist-strain fix (frets angle to match natural hand rotation, reducing the wrist rotation that standard parallel frets force at higher positions). Distinct from short-scale coverage already in the backlog.
- **Adaptive-radius capos** — G7th's Performance 3 ("Adaptive Radius Technology") flexes to match individual fretboard curvature instead of clamping flat; positioned as reducing the squeeze force needed versus a standard capo. New product category, no channel coverage.
- **Forearm/strumming-arm stabilization devices** ("Strum Perfect" and similar) — mentioned in tremor-management research as aids for strumming-arm coordination; a tool category the channel's existing Fix Ladders (partial capo, EBow, lap steel) don't touch.
- **Boutique "ergonomic" guitar marketing surge** — NAMM 2026 coverage pushes ergonomic contouring, weight reduction (~3.2 lb vs ~7.8 lb), and multi-scale necks as premium-tier selling points (Strandberg, Aristides, Kiesel). Directly testable against the channel's core thesis.
- **Format signal:** 2026 YouTube-growth research recommends a 40% discovery Shorts / 40% engagement long-form / 20% community mix, with CTR and retention outweighing upload frequency. The channel's existing "30-second Reels" note (above) is directionally right but not yet a repeatable weekly cadence.

## New Video Ideas

**1. Tremor and the Guitar: What the Research Actually Says** *(Playing Through It)*
- Friction: players with Parkinson's or essential tremor assume playing is over, or feel embarrassed asking.
- Fix angle: cite the actual GuitarPD study; technique (nylon strings, rest strokes) plus equipment.
- Fix Ladder: $0 retune to open tuning + rest-stroke technique → $10–15 nylon or lightest strings → $40–60 forearm/strumming stabilization aid → $100+ full comfort setup with light-tension strings.
- Audience appeal: high — clinically credible, essentially unclaimed territory, strong shareability into Parkinson's communities beyond the guitar niche.

**2. Fanned Frets: The Ergonomic Guitar Nobody Told You About**
- Friction: wrist rotation increases the higher up a standard neck you play, which compounds for anyone managing joint pain.
- Fix angle: explain multi-scale/fanned-fret geometry as a hardware option distinct from short-scale.
- Fix Ladder: $0 thumb-position technique adjustment → $15–20 try-before-you-buy at a local shop → (no meaningful $25–100 tier — say so on camera) → $100+ entry-level fanned-fret guitar.
- Audience appeal: medium-high, especially Comfortable Player persona; genuinely new information for most viewers.

**3. The Keith Richards Guitar Adaptation Breakdown**
- Friction: viewers see a famous player openly discuss adapting to arthritis and want to know if it applies to them.
- Fix angle: reaction/analysis format — translate his reported adaptations ("fewer notes," "other ways around it") into concrete Fix Ladder moves. First topical newsjack format for the channel; verify quotes against original source before filming.
- Fix Ladder: reuses existing arthritis Fix Ladder (light strings → nut/saddle work → full comfort setup), reframed through his specific adaptations.
- Audience appeal: high — timely hook, strong CTR potential, low production cost since it reuses existing setup knowledge.

**4. Are $2,000 Ergonomic Guitars Actually Worth It?**
- Friction: Comfortable Player persona sees boutique "ergonomic" marketing and wonders whether it's real or premium-priced hype.
- Fix angle: side-by-side test — how much of the comfort gap a $25–80 setup closes on a standard guitar versus a boutique ergonomically-contoured instrument.
- Fix Ladder: $0 posture/technique → $10 strap adjustment → $25–80 comfort setup (reuses $4,000 Mistake logic) → $1,500+ boutique ergonomic guitar, no ceiling.
- Audience appeal: medium-high — reinforces the channel's core thesis directly, positions the channel as unafraid to test premium claims honestly.

**5. Capo Showdown: Does "Adaptive Radius" Actually Reduce Hand Strain?**
- Friction: standard capos apply flat, even pressure regardless of fretboard curvature, forcing extra squeeze force — a real cost for arthritic or reduced-grip hands.
- Fix angle: comparison test (standard trigger capo vs. partial capo vs. G7th Performance 3) with a simple felt/measured squeeze-force comparison.
- Fix Ladder: $0 technique (capo placed close behind the fret) → $15–20 Kyser/Shubb → $30–50 standard G7th → $70–100+ G7th Performance 3 (Adaptive Radius).
- Audience appeal: high — comparison format is proven for this channel, affiliate-ready, directly serves the arthritis/reduced-grip audience.

**6. Stroke Recovery and Guitar: Relearning an Old Skill With New Hands** *(Playing Through It)*
- Friction: "stroke recovery" is named as an audience in the Playing Through It series mission but has no dedicated episode yet; one-sided weakness has no guitar-specific resource.
- Fix angle: one-sided weakness adaptations, simplified chord shapes, extending the existing 15-minute sprint into a patience-paced re-entry.
- Fix Ladder: $0 open tuning + one-finger chords → $6 lightest strings → $20–30 partial capo → $100+ short-scale or lap-style setup.
- Audience appeal: medium-high — fills a named-but-empty slot in the channel's own series mission; strong emotional resonance.

## Content Gaps and Underexplored Angles

1. **Parkinson's/tremor** — the single biggest gap found. Real clinical research exists (GuitarPD, PMC-indexed) and no guitar channel is covering it; bigger differentiation opportunity than carpal tunnel, which at least has scattered coverage elsewhere.
2. **Multi-scale/fanned-fret guitars** — the channel's ergonomic-hardware coverage currently stops at short-scale; fanned-fret is a distinct, currently-trending design with no mention in any planning doc.
3. **Newsjacking / reaction format** — the channel has no format for responding to a trending guitar-press story or public figure's comments. Low production cost, high topicality; could become an occasional recurring segment rather than a one-off.
4. **New adaptive tools** — forearm stabilizers and adaptive-radius capos are 2026-era products with zero channel coverage; existing tool references (partial capo, EBow, lap steel) predate them.
5. **Premium/boutique ergonomic guitars** — only one existing premium-guitar video (Ergonomic Heirloom Build) exists; the 2026 NAMM ergonomic-guitar wave is an opening to serve the Comfortable Player persona at the top of the Fix Ladder while testing the marketing claims honestly.
6. **No formal Shorts cadence** — the channel's "before/after moments make natural Reels" observation (above) has never been turned into a repeatable weekly Shorts plan, despite 2026 growth data favoring a discovery-Shorts + engagement-long-form mix over long-form alone.
