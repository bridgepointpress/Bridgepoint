## Content Gaps and Underexplored Angles

1. **Carpal tunnel as a distinct condition** — forums are full of this question; no channel addresses it clearly and directly. High-priority gap.
2. **Seasonal/humidity content** — guitar setups drift in winter. Repeatable, searchable, and builds returning viewer habit.
3. **The luthier vs. DIY decision** — high emotional charge for this audience (cost anxiety + trust issues); the channel is perfectly positioned to answer it neutrally.
4. **"Viewer rescue" format** — invite a subscriber to send their guitar, document the full fix on camera. Builds community, generates social proof, creates shareable content.
5. **Playing during a flare-up** — most adaptive playing content is setup-focused; no one covers what to do on a bad hand day.
6. **Guitar size and body fit** — short-scale, parlour, and 3/4 guitars are covered in gear content but almost never framed as a comfort/ergonomics fix. Strong channel fit.
7. **One-handed and partial-limb playing** — Django is already in the channel DNA. An episode here would be the most shareable PTI video and draw coverage beyond the guitar niche.

## Trending Topics & Keywords (new since June)

- **"Guitar for seniors" / "easiest playing acoustic guitar"** — multiple 2026 buying guides now exist for this exact search ([Guitar Review's 2026 Seniors' Guide](https://guitarreview.net/2026-seniors-guide-finding-the-easiest-playing-acoustic-guitar/), [Best Guitar for Arthritis Hands](https://guitarreview.net/best-guitar-for-arthritis-hands/)). This is now a recognised buying-guide category, not just a forum question — the channel's Fix Ladder framing is well positioned to answer it more honestly than a straight product list.
- **Pre-"re-engineered" guitars as a product category** — brands like [Zager Guitars](https://zagerguitar.com/the-best-guitar-for-arthritis/) market instruments built low-action from the factory specifically for arthritis, small hands, and hand pain, using a flexible-top bracing system and widened string spacing. This is a direct commercial answer to the exact friction the channel addresses through DIY fixes — a natural comparison angle.
- **Silicone finger guards / finger caps** — a small, cheap accessory category (Amazon listings for guitar-specific silicone fingertip protectors, and general finger-arthritis protectors sized for guitar use) that shows real search and purchase volume but no credible review content evaluating whether they help.
- **Essential tremor and guitar** — active forum discussion (Tony's Acoustic Challenge, Telecaster Forum, Delcamp Classical Guitar Forum) but no structured video content. Distinct mechanism from arthritis: involuntary movement between notes rather than joint pain or stiffness, so the fixes are different (anchoring technique, pick weight, timing) rather than string gauge or nut height.
- **Guitarist gift guides** — mainstream 2026 guides (Sweetwater, Guitar World, Guitar Center, GuitarLobby) are publishing now for the holiday season and explicitly steer buyers away from guitars and amps as gifts ("too personal to choose for someone else"). None address the arthritis/hand-changed audience at all — an open lane, and timely: gift-guide content needs to publish in October/November to catch search volume.
- **Artist-driven visibility** — Guitar World's ongoing coverage of Joe Perry and Keith Richards adapting their setups for arthritis is keeping the topic in front of a mainstream guitar audience, which raises the ceiling on search interest for "guitar and arthritis" content generally.

## New Video Ideas

**1. Playing With a Tremor: What Actually Helps**
- Friction: hands move on their own between notes, so chords and picking land inconsistently no matter how much the player's technique improves.
- Fix angle: technique first (anchoring the picking hand, slowing tempo, right-hand approach), then hardware.
- Fix Ladder: Free — anchor the palm or pinky against the body, retime around the tremor rather than fighting the beat / Affordable (£5–15) — heavier pick for more predictable attack, or a light wrist weight some players use to dampen shake / Quality upgrade (£25–35) — partial capo to reduce the number of independently fretted notes per chord / Premium (£100+) — wider string spacing or a short-scale neck that gives more margin for imprecise placement.
- Adaptive relevance: essential tremor is a distinct condition from arthritis and currently has zero dedicated coverage anywhere in the backlog or June research — a genuine "Playing Through It" gap.

**2. The £900 Guitar vs the £25 Fix: Is a Pre-Solved Guitar Worth It?**
- Friction: viewers see guitars marketed as already built for easy playing and don't know whether that's worth paying for over doing the same fixes themselves.
- Fix angle: honest, editorial comparison — measure a re-engineered guitar's action, neck width, and string spacing against a standard guitar taken through the channel's usual setup process.
- Fix Ladder: the video's structure *is* the ladder — Free/Affordable/Quality DIY path on one side, Premium pre-built option on the other, both measured against the same baseline.
- Adaptive relevance: central to the whole video. This is a direct echo of Video 5 in the backlog ("comfort is a setup decision, not a purchase decision") applied to a real, currently-marketed product category, so it extends existing channel DNA rather than introducing a new one.
- Note: this is an editorial comparison, not a sponsorship — if a brand in this category approaches the channel, run it through the Mode 2 checklist before any commercial arrangement.

**3. A Gift Guide for the Guitarist Whose Hands Have Changed**
- Friction: family members want to buy something meaningful for a guitarist with arthritis, tremor, or reduced grip, but generic gift guides actively steer them away from anything guitar-related.
- Fix angle: seasonal buying guide, structured explicitly as the Fix Ladder.
- Fix Ladder: Free — a written voucher for a setup session together / Affordable (£5–15) — finger guards, string lubricant, a peg winder / Quality upgrade (£25–35) — a Graph Tech nut installed as a gift, or a partial capo / Premium (£100+) — a short-scale or re-engineered guitar, framed honestly against Idea 2 above.
- Timing note: needs to publish in October to catch gift-guide search volume before December.

**4. Do Finger Guards Actually Stop the Pain? (Silicone Finger Caps Tested)**
- Friction: new and returning players — not only those with a diagnosed condition — get raw, sore fingertips before calluses form, and some stop playing during that window.
- Fix angle: honest product test, before/after over a practice session.
- Fix Ladder: Free — shorter, more frequent sessions while calluses build / Affordable (£5–8) — silicone finger guard multipack / Quality upgrade (£15–25) — lighter strings or a partial capo to reduce the pressure needed / Premium (£100+) — nylon-string or short-scale guitar as a longer-term answer.
- Adaptive relevance: useful for players with thin or fragile skin from some conditions, but framed as universal — not everyone watching this has a diagnosed impairment, and the video shouldn't imply they need one to want less pain.

**5. Changing Strings When Your Hands Don't Cooperate**
- Friction: players with reduced grip or dexterity dread restringing enough that they leave old, hard-to-tune strings on for months, which quietly makes everything else about the guitar harder to play.
- Fix angle: technique and tool, not a new instrument.
- Fix Ladder: Free — winding technique that doesn't require pinching or twisting / Affordable (£8–12) — a combined string winder and cutter / Quality upgrade (£20–35) — an ergonomic-grip peg winder / Premium (£100+) — locking tuners retrofitted, which removes the winding step almost entirely.
- Adaptive relevance: high. This sits upstream of nearly every other fix on the channel — a player who can't face restringing never gets to the nut, saddle, or string-gauge fixes at all — and nothing in the current backlog addresses it directly.

**6. Capo Shootout: Which One Actually Reduces the Load**
- Friction: players are told to "just use a capo" to get around barre chords, but nobody explains that some capos trade finger strain for a new squeeze-strength requirement.
- Fix angle: comparison/product test.
- Fix Ladder: Free — repositioning technique with a capo already owned / Affordable (£10–15) — basic spring capo / Quality upgrade (£25–35) — squeeze-and-lock or partial capo / Premium (£40+) — multi-position custom capo system.
- Note: partial capos are already referenced as a fix inside roughly six planned videos, but no dedicated comparison exists yet — this would give the channel one clear video to link to instead of re-explaining capo choice each time.

## Content Gaps and Underexplored Angles

1. **Essential tremor** — a distinct mechanism from arthritis (involuntary movement vs. joint pain/stiffness), with real forum demand and zero coverage anywhere in the current plan.
2. **Restringing as a dexterity barrier** — sits upstream of most other Fix Ladder content; a player who can't manage it doesn't reach the fixes the channel already has planned.
3. **The re-engineered/pre-solved guitar category** (Zager and similar) — a live commercial answer to the channel's core question that the channel hasn't yet addressed directly, positive or critical.
4. **Seasonal gift-guide window** — a genuine, time-sensitive publishing opportunity (October–November) with no channel content built for it yet.
5. **Post-stroke playing as its own episode** — named explicitly in the "Playing Through It" series mission but only folded into the broader one-handed/adaptive video (#35); stroke recovery involves different specifics (one-sided weakness, spasticity) than general adaptive playing and may be thin as currently scoped.
6. **Fingertip pain during the callus-building window** — affects nearly every new or returning player, not only those with a named condition; currently no video owns this despite it being one of the most common reasons beginners quit in the first two weeks.
